Abandoned But
Not Forgotten

An interactive educational archive that tells the stories of NASA equipment left behind on the Moon, Mars and beyond — built by a team in Uganda for secondary school learners across East Africa.

ChallengeAbandoned but not Forgotten
Team locationKampala, Uganda
Target audienceS3–S5 secondary school learners
FormatInteractive web app

The problem we're solving

Space science education in Uganda — and across much of East Africa — relies heavily on abstract textbook content with no connection to real missions, real data or real questions scientists are still working on. Students learn about Newton's laws but never see how they apply to orbital mechanics. They study ecosystems but never encounter the question of whether ancient Mars had one.

Meanwhile, NASA has left hardware on other worlds that most people don't know exists — a seismometer still waiting for moonquakes it will never detect, a rover that survived 59× longer than planned before going silent in a dust storm, a spacecraft that left the solar system and is still phoning home. These are extraordinary stories. They're not being told to the people who need them most.

How it addresses the challenge

Narrative depth

Every piece of equipment has a 7-chapter story: the dream behind it, the journey, the mission, the challenges, why it was left, the evidence it returned, and what its legacy means now. Plus a first-person voice — the machine speaking.

Personal connection

Each mission is linked to a real, local Ugandan context. Opportunity's dust story connects to solar panel maintenance at rural health centres. Cassini's rings connect to Uganda's physics curriculum on wave behaviour.

Scientific accuracy

All mission data, instrument descriptions and discoveries are sourced from NASA primary sources, linked directly within each equipment record. Evidence cards in the investigation section are marked as teaching simulations.

Age-appropriate + accessible

Targeted at S3–S5 (ages 15–18). Content is structured around Uganda's secondary curriculum. No login required. Runs on a basic laptop with a local server — no cloud dependency for core features.

Engaging format

3D model viewer for Opportunity, InSight and Cassini. Interactive surface maps showing exact landing coordinates. Virtual science investigation where learners examine evidence before seeing the conclusion.

Swahili localisation

Key science terms translated to Swahili in the Uganda section of each mission. Machine translation (Helsinki-NLP) with a glossary display — acknowledges the tool and encourages discussion of translation limits.

What's in the app

EQUIPMENT ARCHIVE

10 mission records with filter by world, text search and semantic deep search. Each card opens a full detail page.

MISSION STORIES

7-chapter narrative per mission. First-person voice from the machine. NASA original image gallery. Source links to NASA primary documentation.

SCIENCE LAB

6 learning pathways across instruments, discoveries and scientific methods — built around the specific measurements each mission returned.

VIRTUAL INVESTIGATION

Learner examines evidence cards, records observations, picks a hypothesis, submits reasoning — then sees how it compares to the actual scientific conclusion.

SURFACE MAPS

Interactive Moon and Mars maps with clickable markers at exact mission coordinates. Clicking a marker shows the mission brief and links to the full story.

MISSION TIMELINES

Launch → travel → landing → mission highlights → silence. Per-mission, selectable from a dropdown.

QUIZ + LEADERBOARD

4-question quiz with server-side answer validation. Score submission to a SQLite leaderboard. Answers never sent to the client.

UGANDA CONNECTION

Every mission has a local activity tied to Uganda's curriculum with Swahili glossary. Opportunity → solar dust. Viking → soil chemistry. Cassini → wave physics.

3D MODELS

NASA-sourced GLB models for Opportunity, InSight and Cassini, displayed in-browser via model-viewer with orbit controls.

Technical stack

Deliberately minimal. No paid APIs for core features. Runs locally on a basic laptop — important for schools in Uganda with unreliable internet.

Python 3.10+ FastAPI SQLite Vanilla JS sentence-transformers Helsinki-NLP translation Three.js model-viewer NASA Image API

NASA image gallery and Swahili translation require internet. Everything else — archive, science lab, investigation, quiz, 3D models — runs fully offline once installed.

What we'd build next

Teacher mode with class progress tracking. Audio narration for the first-person machine voices. Expanded archive with Apollo surface experiments, Surveyor landers and Spirit's full story. Offline-first PWA packaging so the whole app installs as a school resource without a Python server.
